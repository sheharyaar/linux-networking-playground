Epilogue: traps, fast paths, what came next

~RT16 min · LAB · Spine: our skb has been born in a ring, delivered to a socket, sent through a loopback device, a veth pair, a bridge, a bond, a VRF, two kinds of upper device, an INPUT-hook load balancer and a BPF datapath. This chapter puts all twelve journeys in one table, then names the machinery that is quietly replacing parts of them.

The twelve scenarios, end to end

Each row is the ordered list of stations the skb actually visits. Capitals are netfilter hooks; monospace is a function you can probe.

Three patterns cover almost all of it. A device either consumes the skb and re-queues it elsewhere, as the bridge and the BPF redirect do; or relabels it and sends the core round again, as the bond, VRF, macvlan and ipvlan do; or hands it to a receive function from inside a transmit function, as loopback and veth do. The rest is which field is read to choose.

Four pieces of machinery that change these answers

The flowtable. nf_flow_offload_ip_hook (net/netfilter/nf_flow_table_ip.c:807) sits on the netdev ingress hook, ahead of everything in the chart. Once a forwarded connection has been offloaded, later packets are matched, rewritten and transmitted from that hook, so FORWARD and conntrack are skipped for the rest of the connection. The symptom is rule counters that stop climbing while traffic continues.

tcx. Chapter 3 met it; the ordering is worth pinning down. In sch_handle_ingress, tcx_run runs at net/core/dev.c:4486 and the legacy tc_run at line 4490 executes only if tcx answered TC_ACT_UNSPEC. tcx programs therefore run first, attach as links, and several can share one device without clobbering each other.

netkit. The device Cilium moves to after veth. netkit_xmit (drivers/net/netkit.c:92) runs a BPF program inside the transmit function and then calls __netif_rx for the peer at line 121, one step earlier than a tc hook on a veth host end, removing a device from the path.

BIG TCP and the ingress hook. gso_ipv4_max_size (include/linux/netdevice.h:2168) carries segmentation-offload sizes past the old 64 KB ceiling, cutting how often everything above runs at all. NF_INET_INGRESS (include/uapi/linux/netfilter.h:49) gives nftables a hook at device ingress, before ip_rcv, where tc and XDP already were.

What this document did not establish

Being honest about the gaps is more useful than pretending there are none.

  • Unix sockets were not traced. pwru filters on IP headers and a unix skb has none; the node has no bpftrace. The bpftrace block in that lab is labelled expected.
  • IPVS was not exercised. ipvsadm is absent and installing it was out of scope, so no virtual service existed. The ip_vs module does load on this kernel; it was loaded once, found inert, and unloaded.
  • br_netfilter was not loaded. Its hooks register globally and the sysctl defaults to on, which would have changed packet handling for the bridge carrying the live cluster. The plain bridge path was measured; that half is from the source.
  • Host labs were not run. Host-side work needs a root password, so those labs are written for you to run and their expected output is marked.
  • VXLAN and native routing were not measured. Both cluster configurations are in lab/ and were deliberately not created.
  • Diagrams were never rendered. No browser on the bench. Their source was checked mechanically for the header line, entity escaping and participant aliases, and nothing more.

One habit is worth taking away. Every claim here that turned out to be wrong was wrong the same way: a name or a line number remembered from an older kernel. The IPVS hooks were renamed, skb_scrub_packet does not clear what folklore says it clears, and a line number in the routing chapter was off by one. Checking a citation costs one sed -n.

Recap

Twelve scenarios reduce to three mechanisms: consume the skb and re-queue it elsewhere, relabel it and restart the core, or call a receive function from inside a transmit function. The netfilter chart is still an accurate map of the stack's middle, and four newer pieces of machinery give packets ways around it: the flowtable skips FORWARD after the first packet, tcx runs before legacy tc and attaches as a link, netkit runs BPF inside the transmit function and removes a device, and BIG TCP reduces how often any of this runs at all. What this document did not measure is listed above rather than glossed.

New words in this chapter

flowtable
A netdev-ingress hook that rewrites and transmits packets of an already-seen forwarded connection, skipping FORWARD and conntrack.
netkit
A device pair that runs a BPF program inside its transmit function and delivers to the peer with __netif_rx.
BIG TCP
Raising the segmentation-offload size limit past 64 KB so fewer, larger units traverse the stack.
NF_INET_INGRESS
A netfilter hook at device ingress, before ip_rcv, at the point tc and XDP already occupied.

Check yourself

1. Group these by the mechanism they use, and give the one function or verdict that identifies each group: bridge, bond, macvlan, veth, loopback, Cilium's same-node redirect.

Answer

Three groups. Consume and re-queue: the bridge, identified by RX_HANDLER_CONSUMED followed by br_dev_queue_push_xmit, and Cilium's redirect, identified by skb_do_redirect returning -EAGAIN. Relabel and restart the core: bond and macvlan, identified by RX_HANDLER_ANOTHER and the another_round label at net/core/dev.c:6017. Receive from inside transmit: veth and loopback, identified by __netif_rx being called from veth_xmit and loopback_xmit.

The near miss is putting Cilium's redirect in the second group because it also reaches another_round. It does, but by a different route: the tc hook's TC_ACT_REDIRECT case, not a receive handler verdict, and it changes the namespace as well as the device.

2. Predict the difference: the same pod-to-pod connection on a node using netkit instead of veth. Which functions from chapter 14's trace disappear, and which single one replaces them?

Answer

veth_xmit, __dev_forward_skb, __dev_forward_skb2 and the enqueue_to_backlog that follows them disappear, and netkit_xmit (drivers/net/netkit.c:92) replaces them. The BPF program runs inside the transmit function rather than at a tc hook on the far side of the pair, so the skb is not handed to the host device first; netkit_xmit calls __netif_rx for the peer directly at line 121. Chapter 14's capture counted one veth_xmit and one enqueue_to_backlog per packet, and both are what this removes.

The near miss is "nothing changes, it is still a device pair". The pair remains, but the program's position moves one step earlier, and that is exactly the step being saved.
