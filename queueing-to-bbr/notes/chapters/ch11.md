## Errata and since-the-paper notes
Shipped (rendered pages checked):
- **The paper's gap (policer confound), `p.erratum` in the policer learning.** Verdict: a missing explanation. Eq. 3 (p. 134) is sound arithmetic; for the default gain its 20% coincides with BBRv1's policer detector, which Cao invoke one sentence later only for BBR_1.5. The detector tests lost/delivered ≥ 50/256 = 19.53% (`tcp_bbr.c:188, 740–744`; comment says "> 20%"), and since `tp->lost` counts every loss including lost retransmits (`tcp_input.c:1337–1346`), uniform random loss p gives lost/delivered = p/(1−p): the test is crossed at 50/306 = 16.34% of packets sent. Eq. 3 with v7.2's 1% pacing margin (since v4.20) is 19.2%; on Cao's 4.15 it is 20%. Fig. 7a's samples at ≈18% and ≈26% bracket both. Only BBR_1.1 separates the rules (84 Mbit/s already at ≈11.5%, collapsed by ≈18%), so Eq. 3 rests on BBR_1.1 alone. Not a coincidence the authors missed entirely; not a slip. Observation added: a sender probing at 1.25× a policer's rate loses 1 − 1/1.25 = 20% in the probe, the same algebra; the code does not document why 50/256. The detector also needs two consecutive intervals with delivery rates within 1/8 (`:664–669`), so a crossed threshold is not a fired mode; Fig. 7a's 93 Mbit/s at 18% suggests it often did not fire there.
- **The paper's slips (Cao):** p. 134 says CUBIC gets "3× more bandwidth than BBR" at 10 MB; Fig. 8a's end points are ≈810 vs ≈140 Mbit/s (≈6×, read at 400 dpi). Table 1 header (p. 135) prints 1e6 twice; the fifth column (between 5e6 and 5e7) must be 1e7.
- Not shipped as errata, mentioned in prose: the abstract's "large queue sizes" (p. 130) has no queue or RTT measurement behind it; the decision tree's two RTT splits end in same-class leaves; "latency" = completion time; Ware's primer says 8 RTTs for the bandwidth filter (kernel 10 rounds, `tcp_bbr.c:134`); Ware's model overshoots 1 BBR flow at 2–8 BDP (own Fig. 8a).
- Held: Cao vs Ware disagree at 4 BDP on 1 Gbit/s, 20 ms (model 61% BBR, Cao ≈15%); cut from the page for budget. Fig. 7a's 93 Mbit/s at 18% loss (> 82) shows drops before the rate limit and BBR sending above the link; cause of the above-link estimate unresolved (retransmissions are paced in 4.15).
