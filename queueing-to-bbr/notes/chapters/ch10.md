## Spine and strands
Spine: one flow on a 10 Mbit/s, 40 ms path, the paper's own example (Figs 4, 6, 7) and the bench's: BtlBw 9.56 Mbit/s of payload (825.6 segments/s), RTprop 41.2 ms for a full-size packet, BDP 34.0 segments (49 KB), buffer 50 → queue full at 84 in flight. Capstone line kept: "BBR models its path (BtlBw 10 Mbit/s, RTprop 40 ms) and paces by gain".

- `s-two-numbers` — Two numbers and the point between them
- `s-one-sided-filters` — Each estimate errs one way
- `s-pacing-is-the-control` — A rate from the model, a cap from cwnd
- `s-gain-cycle` — A quarter up, a quarter down
- `s-states` — Startup, Drain and ProbeRTT

## Errata and since-the-paper notes
- Erratum shipped (verified on the rendered p. 64): "one loss per 30 million packets for a 10Gbps/100ms path". 1/BDP² at 83,333 packets is 1 per 6.9×10⁹ (4.6×10⁹ with C² = 1.5); RFC 3649 §1 independently says 5×10⁹. 30 M needs BDP 5,477 packets (22.8 KB each at 10 Gbps); the figure fits 657–805 Mbit/s at 100 ms, i.e. about 1 Gbit/s (46–69 M). This confirms the NOTES errata candidate.
- Not shipped (noted only): Fig. 5's axis says "Inflight (Kb)" where the values are KB (50 at 10 Mbps/40 ms); "Delayed and stretched aks." typo p. 66; pull-quote "anomolies" p. 61. None matter to the argument.
- Since: upstream v7.2 tcp_bbr.c is still the 2016 algorithm (header 2–59); sampler moved out of tcp_rate.c in v7.0; IETF draft -06 (6 Jul 2026) specifies a third version out of tree (google/bbr `bbr-v3-2026-09-16-01`): ProbeRTT ≤ every 5 s, cwnd gain 0.5, min_rtt window still 10 s; sampler = min(send rate, ack rate) §4.1.1.2.4.
